# Iron-Deficiency Anemia

## Overview
Anemia is a condition in which the blood lacks enough healthy red blood cells or hemoglobin to carry adequate oxygen to tissues. Iron-deficiency anemia is the most common type of anemia worldwide. Hemoglobin below 13.5 g/dL in men or below 12 g/dL in non-pregnant women is generally considered anemia.

## Causes
Causes include blood loss (heavy menstrual periods, gastrointestinal bleeding from ulcers, polyps, or colon cancer), inadequate dietary iron, poor absorption (celiac disease, gastric bypass surgery), and increased needs during pregnancy. In men and postmenopausal women, iron-deficiency anemia should prompt evaluation for gastrointestinal blood loss.

## Symptoms
Symptoms include fatigue, weakness, pale skin, shortness of breath with exertion, dizziness, cold hands and feet, brittle nails, headache, and pica, which is a craving for non-food items such as ice (pagophagia) or dirt. Restless legs syndrome is also associated with iron deficiency.

## Diagnosis
A complete blood count shows low hemoglobin and small red blood cells (low mean corpuscular volume, or microcytic anemia). Serum ferritin, which reflects iron stores, is the most useful test; a ferritin level below 30 ng/mL strongly suggests iron deficiency. Iron studies typically also show low serum iron, high total iron-binding capacity (TIBC), and low transferrin saturation.

## Treatment
Oral iron supplements such as ferrous sulfate are first-line. Taking iron every other day may improve absorption and reduce side effects. Vitamin C enhances iron absorption, while calcium, tea, coffee, and antacids reduce it. Common side effects of oral iron include constipation, nausea, and dark or black stools. Hemoglobin usually begins rising within 2 to 4 weeks; treatment typically continues for about 3 months after hemoglobin normalizes to replenish stores. Intravenous iron is used when oral iron is not tolerated or not absorbed. Iron-rich foods include red meat, poultry, fish, beans, lentils, tofu, spinach, and iron-fortified cereals.
