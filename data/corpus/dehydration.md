# Dehydration

## Overview
Dehydration occurs when the body loses more fluid than it takes in. Common causes include vomiting, diarrhea, fever, excessive sweating, increased urination (as in uncontrolled diabetes), and not drinking enough. Infants, young children, and older adults are most vulnerable; older adults often have a diminished sense of thirst.

## Symptoms
Mild to moderate dehydration causes thirst, dry mouth, decreased urination, dark yellow urine, headache, fatigue, and dizziness. In infants and young children, signs include no wet diapers for 3 hours or more, crying without tears, a sunken soft spot (fontanelle) on the head, and unusual sleepiness. Severe dehydration may cause extreme thirst, very little or no urine, rapid heartbeat, rapid breathing, sunken eyes, confusion, and fainting, and is a medical emergency.

## Treatment
Mild dehydration can be treated by drinking fluids. For dehydration from vomiting or diarrhea, oral rehydration solutions (ORS) containing a balanced mix of water, glucose, and electrolytes such as sodium and potassium are preferred over plain water, sports drinks, or juice; sugary drinks can worsen diarrhea. Small, frequent sips are better tolerated when nauseated. Severe dehydration requires intravenous (IV) fluids.

## Prevention
Drink fluids regularly throughout the day, more during hot weather, exercise, or illness. Pale yellow urine is a simple indicator of adequate hydration.
