# Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

## Overview
A urinary tract infection is an infection in any part of the urinary system. Most UTIs involve the lower tract: the bladder (cystitis) and urethra. Escherichia coli (E. coli) causes about 75-95% of uncomplicated UTIs. Women are at much greater risk than men because they have a shorter urethra.

## Symptoms
Lower UTI symptoms include a strong, persistent urge to urinate, burning with urination (dysuria), frequent passing of small amounts of urine, cloudy or strong-smelling urine, blood in the urine, and pelvic or suprapubic pain. Older adults may present with confusion. Fever, chills, flank (back or side) pain, nausea, and vomiting suggest a kidney infection (pyelonephritis), which is more serious and needs prompt medical care.

## Diagnosis
Diagnosis is based on symptoms and a urinalysis showing white blood cells, nitrites, or leukocyte esterase. A urine culture identifies the bacteria and guides antibiotic choice and is recommended for complicated or recurrent infections.

## Treatment
Uncomplicated cystitis in women is treated with antibiotics. First-line options include nitrofurantoin for 5 days, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for 3 days (where local resistance is low), and a single dose of fosfomycin. Fluoroquinolones are generally reserved for other infections because of side effects. Phenazopyridine may relieve burning but turns urine orange.

## Prevention
Prevention measures include drinking plenty of fluids, urinating after sexual intercourse, wiping front to back, and avoiding irritating feminine products. Vaginal estrogen can reduce recurrent UTIs in postmenopausal women.
