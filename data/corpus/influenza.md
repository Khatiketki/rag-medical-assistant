# Influenza (Flu)

## Overview
Influenza is a contagious respiratory illness caused by influenza A and B viruses. It spreads mainly through respiratory droplets when infected people cough, sneeze, or talk. Adults are typically contagious from about 1 day before symptoms begin until 5 to 7 days after becoming sick.

## Symptoms
Flu usually starts suddenly with fever or feeling feverish, chills, cough, sore throat, runny or stuffy nose, muscle or body aches, headache, and fatigue. Vomiting and diarrhea are more common in children. Unlike the common cold, the onset of flu is abrupt and fatigue and body aches are more severe.

## Prevention
Annual flu vaccination is recommended for everyone 6 months of age and older who does not have a contraindication. It takes about two weeks after vaccination for protective antibodies to develop. Frequent handwashing, covering coughs, and staying home when sick also reduce spread.

## Treatment
Most people recover within one to two weeks with rest and fluids. Antiviral drugs such as oseltamivir (Tamiflu), zanamivir, peramivir, and baloxavir can shorten illness and reduce complications. Antivirals work best when started within 48 hours of symptom onset, and they are recommended for people who are hospitalized or at high risk of complications. Antibiotics do not work against influenza viruses.

## High-Risk Groups
People at higher risk of complications include adults 65 and older, children younger than 5 (especially under 2), pregnant people, residents of long-term care facilities, and people with chronic conditions such as asthma, diabetes, heart disease, or weakened immune systems.

## Emergency Warning Signs
In adults, seek emergency care for difficulty breathing or shortness of breath, persistent chest or abdominal pain or pressure, persistent dizziness or confusion, inability to arouse, seizures, not urinating, or severe muscle pain.
