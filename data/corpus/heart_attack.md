# Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction)

## Overview
A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is blocked, usually by a blood clot forming on a ruptured cholesterol plaque in a coronary artery. Without prompt treatment, the heart muscle begins to die. Call 911 immediately if a heart attack is suspected; do not drive yourself to the hospital.

## Symptoms
The most common symptom is chest pain or discomfort, often described as pressure, squeezing, fullness, or tightness in the center or left side of the chest, lasting more than a few minutes or going away and coming back. Pain may spread to the arm (especially the left), jaw, neck, back, or stomach. Other symptoms include shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness, and unusual fatigue. Women, older adults, and people with diabetes are more likely to have atypical symptoms such as shortness of breath, nausea, back or jaw pain, and fatigue without prominent chest pain.

## What to Do
Call 911. While waiting, emergency dispatchers may advise chewing a regular 325 mg aspirin (unless allergic or told not to by a doctor), because it helps prevent further clotting. If the person becomes unresponsive and is not breathing normally, start CPR and use an automated external defibrillator (AED) if available.

## Diagnosis and Treatment
Diagnosis uses an electrocardiogram (ECG) and blood tests for cardiac troponin. In an ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), the goal is to open the blocked artery with percutaneous coronary intervention (angioplasty and stenting) ideally within 90 minutes of first medical contact. Long-term treatment often includes aspirin, a P2Y12 inhibitor, a high-intensity statin, a beta-blocker, and an ACE inhibitor, plus cardiac rehabilitation.

## Risk Factors
Risk factors include high blood pressure, high LDL cholesterol, smoking, diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity, family history of early heart disease, and older age.
