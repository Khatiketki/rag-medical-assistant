# Migraine

## Overview
Migraine is a neurological disorder characterized by recurrent, moderate to severe headaches. Migraine attacks typically last from 4 to 72 hours if untreated. Migraine is about three times more common in women than in men.

## Symptoms
A migraine headache is often one-sided and pulsating, and worsens with routine physical activity. It is commonly accompanied by nausea, vomiting, and sensitivity to light (photophobia) and sound (phonophobia). About one-quarter to one-third of people with migraine experience an aura: temporary visual disturbances such as flashing lights, zigzag lines, or blind spots, or tingling, that usually develop over 5 minutes and last up to 60 minutes before the headache.

## Triggers
Common triggers include stress, hormonal changes such as menstruation, irregular sleep, skipped meals, dehydration, alcohol (especially red wine), caffeine withdrawal, bright lights, and strong smells. A headache diary can help identify personal triggers.

## Acute Treatment
For mild to moderate attacks, NSAIDs (such as ibuprofen or naproxen), acetaminophen, or combination analgesics with caffeine are used. Triptans (such as sumatriptan and rizatriptan) are migraine-specific drugs for moderate to severe attacks; they are not recommended for people with coronary artery disease or uncontrolled hypertension. Newer options include gepants (such as ubrogepant and rimegepant) and lasmiditan. Using acute medication on 10 to 15 or more days per month can cause medication-overuse headache.

## Preventive Treatment
Prevention is considered for people with frequent or disabling attacks (for example, 4 or more headache days per month). Options include beta-blockers (propranolol, metoprolol), topiramate, amitriptyline, candesartan, CGRP monoclonal antibodies (such as erenumab), and onabotulinumtoxinA for chronic migraine.

## When to Seek Emergency Care
Seek immediate care for a sudden, severe "thunderclap" headache (the worst headache of one's life), headache with fever and stiff neck, headache after a head injury, or headache with weakness, confusion, or trouble speaking.
